The problem

Access isn't adoption

A tool licensed to 100,000 employees doesn't change how work gets done. Some people become power users quickly; others try the tools once and fall back to old habits — the "jagged edge." The difference is rarely access. It's whether a person can see, in the context of their own work, where AI helps them do something better, faster, or with more confidence.

We start with the role, not the tool.— Persona Acceleration principle, Frontier Firm Playbook

How we do it

Five moves that turn trials into habits

01

Make the role visible

We build a persona-level view of pain points, unmet needs and high-value moments — including how time is spent across internal meetings, admin, focus blocks and customer-facing work — then set benchmarks for how time should be spent.

02

Map AI to real moments

We don't ask people to "use AI more." We show them where it fits — preparing customer materials, updating CRM, analyzing performance data, generating deal packages, summarizing meetings, prioritizing pipeline.

03

Role-based learning loops

Small-group huddles work because they are specific, social and practical. People see peers applying AI to recognizable work, ask questions safely, and repeat the behavior.

04

Engage managers visibly

Manager role-modeling is the single biggest predictor of adoption. We make AI usage part of the operating rhythm — not an optional side activity.

05

Instrument the change

We use huddle engagement, usage, feedback, surveys and outcome indicators to see whether adoption is turning into habit formation and better work.
